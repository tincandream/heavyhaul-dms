Using Heavy Haul Command

Where to go in the management system and when to use each workspace.

Welcome

The Welcome page is the starting point after sign-in. Use it to review priorities, continue current work and see recent activity.

Open a specific load from Fleet Board or All Loads. The Load Workspace is the operational file for that shipment.

  • Overview — core load information and status.

  • Permits — permit requirements and status.
  • Escorts — pilot car and escort assignments.
  • Checklist — load-specific operational checklist.
  • Expenses — load expenses and cost tracking.
  • Documents — rate cons, BOLs, PODs, permits, photos and other load files.
  • Notes — dispatcher notes for the shipment.

Before Sourcing

Know the carrier before looking for freight.

Before searching for a load, know what the carrier can and will haul. This prevents wasted calls and bad bookings.

Know the Carrier

  • Truck and trailer type.
  • Trailer capacity.
  • Preferred freight.
  • Freight the carrier refuses.
  • Maximum preferred dimensions and weight.
  • Preferred lanes and states.
  • States or regions to avoid.
  • Minimum load revenue.
  • Target rate per mile.
  • Payment-term preferences.
  • Who normally handles permits.
  • Who normally handles escorts.
  • Whether carrier approval is required before booking.
SYSTEM: Fleet Command → Fleet Setup

Screening a Load

Decide whether an opportunity deserves a broker call.

Initial Screen

  • Commodity.
  • Loaded dimensions.
  • Gross weight.
  • Required equipment.
  • Pickup and delivery locations.
  • Pickup and delivery windows.
  • Deadhead to pickup.
  • Driver availability and legal hours.
  • Likely permits and escorts.
  • Rate and payment terms.

Profitability Is More Than RPM

Consider deadhead, permit costs, escort costs, tolls, fuel, complexity of the move, driver availability, market conditions and possible return freight.

Screen → Estimate Profit → Negotiate → Compare to Carrier Requirements → Book or Pass
SYSTEM: Sourcing → Opportunities → Calculators
04

Broker Calls & Negotiation

Gather the real scope of the move before agreeing to the rate.

Confirm These Items

  • Is the load still available?
  • Pickup location and window.
  • Delivery location and appointment.
  • Commodity.
  • Length, width and height.
  • Gross weight.
  • Required equipment.
  • Who handles permits?
  • Who handles escorts?
  • Detention terms.
  • Layover terms.
  • TONU terms.
  • Payment terms.
  • Rate.

Negotiation

Understand the scope first. Negotiate after you know the freight, equipment requirements, dimensions, permitting and escort responsibilities.

Useful phrasing: “What is the best you can do on the rate?” or “We’re going to need $___ to make this work.”
Do not accept a rate simply because the RPM looks attractive. Heavy haul costs and operational complexity can change the value of the load significantly.
05

Booking a Load

Turn the broker agreement into a controlled operational load file.

Before Booking

  • Confirm the carrier accepts the load when approval is required.
  • Verify the equipment is appropriate.
  • Verify dimensions and weight.
  • Confirm pickup and delivery appointments.
  • Confirm permit responsibility.
  • Confirm escort responsibility.
  • Review detention, layover and TONU terms.
  • Make sure the Rate Confirmation matches the verbal agreement.
SYSTEM: Dispatch → New Load

After the Rate Confirmation

Save the signed or final Rate Confirmation to the load's Documents tab so it remains part of that shipment's record.

SYSTEM: Load Workspace → Documents
06

HOS & Driver Availability

Use legal availability, appointment timing and real-world travel demands together.

Planning Mindset

  1. Ask driver availability first.
  2. Review the driver's current ELD clocks.
  3. Compare hours to deadhead and pickup timing.
  4. Allow for loading, securement, traffic and delays.
  5. Negotiate realistic appointments.
  6. Never pressure the driver to violate HOS.
7–11 Hours Generally favorable for same-day pickup, depending on deadhead and appointment timing.
4–7 Hours Conditional. Evaluate distance, loading time, traffic and appointment windows carefully.
Under 4 Hours Generally unfavorable for same-day pickup. Look for a later appointment or post-reset option.
The ELD is the operational source of truth. The HOS calculator is a planning aid, not a replacement for the driver's actual logs.
SYSTEM: Sourcing → Calculators → HOS Calculator
07

Permits

First determine who owns the responsibility, then track it until the load is legal to move.

First Question

Who is responsible for obtaining the permits for this move?

Responsibility may belong to the carrier, broker, dispatcher, permit service or another authorized party. Never assume.

Track

  • States requiring permits.
  • Permit application status.
  • Permit issuance.
  • Approved route.
  • Travel days and hours.
  • Curfews or restricted travel periods.
  • Escort requirements.
  • Special instructions.
  • Permit expiration or travel window.
A route change on an oversize load may require permit review or revision. Do not casually reroute a permitted load.
SYSTEM: Load Workspace → Permits

Escorts

Determine who is coordinating the escorts and keep every party on the same schedule.

Possible Requirements

  • No escort.
  • Front escort.
  • Rear escort.
  • Front and rear escorts.
  • High-pole escort.
  • Police escort.
  • Utility escort.

Before Calling an Escort Company

  • Commodity.
  • Length, width and height.
  • Gross weight.
  • Pickup and delivery locations.
  • States traveled.
  • Planned route.
  • Travel dates.
  • Driver name and phone.
  • Truck number if available.

Confirm

  • Number and type of escort vehicles.
  • Availability.
  • Escort driver's name and phone.
  • Meeting location.
  • Meeting time.
  • Pricing.
  • Any route concerns.
SYSTEM: Load Workspace → Escorts

Route & State Restrictions

Heavy haul routing is a compliance issue, not just a navigation issue.

Review

  • Permit-approved route.
  • Bridge restrictions.
  • Tunnel restrictions.
  • Construction.
  • Height and clearance concerns.
  • Weight restrictions.
  • Travel-hour restrictions.
  • Curfews.
  • Weekend and holiday restrictions.
  • Escort requirements by state.
If the permitted route becomes unavailable, verify the legal alternate route before instructing the driver to move.

10

Pre-Departure

Make sure the load, driver and paperwork are ready before travel begins.

  • Driver accepted the load.
  • Driver has sufficient legal hours.
  • Pickup information is correct.
  • Driver has shipper contact/check-in information.
  • Required permits are available.
  • Driver understands permit restrictions.
  • Escorts are confirmed.
  • Escort contacts have been exchanged.
  • Meeting locations and times are confirmed.
  • Required banners, flags or warning devices are ready.
  • Load securement is confirmed.
SYSTEM: Load Workspace → Checklist
11

Pickup

Confirm the driver gets loaded, receives the required paperwork and can legally begin the move.

Before Arrival

  • Confirm pickup appointment.
  • Confirm freight is expected to be ready.
  • Confirm gate/check-in instructions.
  • Communicate PPE or site requirements.
  • Confirm loading instructions.

After Loading

  • Confirm successful pickup.
  • Confirm cargo dimensions if they changed.
  • Confirm load is secured.
  • Confirm BOL and required paperwork are received.
  • Confirm permits are available.
  • Confirm escort coordination.
  • Update the broker when appropriate.
If loading takes longer than expected, document the waiting time and review detention terms.
12

En Route

Maintain useful communication while allowing the driver to focus on safe operation.

Driver Support

  • Check progress at reasonable intervals.
  • Confirm the driver remains on the permitted route.
  • Relay important broker updates.
  • Help coordinate safe parking if requested.
  • Assist with locating fuel, repairs or tire service if requested.
  • Coordinate roadside assistance when requested by the carrier.
  • Remind the driver of appointments or escort meeting points.

Broker Communication

  • Confirm pickup.
  • Provide meaningful status updates.
  • Report significant delays promptly.
  • Update ETA when needed.
  • Report mechanical or weather delays.
  • Confirm when travel resumes.
  • Confirm arrival and delivery.
Do not overwhelm the driver with unnecessary calls. Communicate when information is operationally useful.
13

Weigh Stations & Inspections

Treat a weigh-station or inspection stop as an operational event that may affect schedule and compliance.

If the Driver Is Stopped

  1. Confirm the driver is safely stopped.
  2. Ask what type of stop or inspection is occurring.
  3. Do not interfere with the driver's interaction with enforcement.
  4. Confirm whether paperwork, permit or weight issues were identified.
  5. Document meaningful information in the load record.
  6. Notify the carrier and broker if the stop affects the schedule.
  7. Update ETA when necessary.

Possible Documents to Retain

  • Scale tickets.
  • Inspection paperwork.
  • Permit-related documents.
  • Receipts.
  • Photos when appropriate.
SYSTEM: Load Workspace → Timeline / Notes / Documents
14

Problems, Holds & Delays

The dispatcher coordinates the response, communication and revised plan.

Use This Mental Checklist

  1. Is everyone safe?
  2. Who needs to know?
  3. What information do they need?
  4. What resource or service can be coordinated?
  5. What is the updated plan?

Road Closure

  • Keep the driver on the legal/permitted route unless an alternate route is approved.
  • Contact permit support if a route revision may be necessary.
  • Notify the broker.
  • Update the receiver and ETA if needed.

Mechanical Breakdown

  • Confirm the driver is in a safe location.
  • Notify the broker if schedule is affected.
  • Help locate service if the carrier requests assistance.
  • Track repair progress.
  • Update ETA.

Weather Delay

  • Do not pressure the driver to continue unsafely.
  • Notify the broker.
  • Review permit/travel restrictions if needed.
  • Coordinate safe waiting/parking.
  • Update ETA.

Escort Does Not Arrive

  • Contact the escort company.
  • Verify meeting location and time.
  • Notify the broker when the delay affects the load.
  • Coordinate the revised schedule.
SYSTEM: Fleet Board → Open Load → Timeline / Notes
15

Delivery

Confirm successful delivery and obtain the paperwork needed to close the load.

Before Delivery

  • Confirm delivery appointment.
  • Confirm receiver/check-in instructions.
  • Confirm unloading requirements.
  • Notify broker of significant schedule changes.

After Delivery

  • Confirm the freight was delivered.
  • Obtain signed POD.
  • Obtain other required delivery documents.
  • Document detention or unloading delays.
  • Notify the broker.
The operational move may be over, but the dispatch file is not complete until the required paperwork has been received.

17

Dispatcher Red Flags

Stop and verify before proceeding when critical information is missing or conflicts.

  • Missing dimensions.
  • Missing weight.
  • Wrong trailer for the load.
  • No permit plan for an oversize load.
  • Driver unavailable.
  • Driver lacks sufficient legal hours.
  • Pickup or delivery cannot realistically be met.
  • Rate Confirmation differs from negotiated terms.
  • Permit restrictions conflict with the schedule.
  • Required escorts are not arranged.
  • A route change is proposed without permit review.

The 10 C's of Heavy Haul Dispatch

A quick mental check before and during every heavy haul move.

1 · Carrier Is the correct carrier, truck and trailer assigned?
2 · Cargo Do you know the commodity, dimensions and weight?
3 · Contract Does the Rate Confirmation match the agreement?
4 · Clock Does the driver have sufficient legal HOS?
5 · Compliance Are permits and regulations addressed?
6 · Coordination Are escorts and appointments confirmed?
7 · Communication Have the driver, broker and facilities been informed?
8 · Conditions Have weather, construction and route conditions been considered?
9 · Confirmation Is the driver prepared before moving?
10 · Completion Was delivery confirmed and the required paperwork submitted?
